## Who This Track Is For

K-12 administrators and teachers deciding how artificial intelligence enters a classroom, and parents of autistic students who are navigating a support system with a hard edge on it.

The two populations are in one track because they face the same underlying question: whether a system built for averages can be made to see an individual.

---

## The Structural Problem

In education, AI is arriving as either a threat to teaching or a replacement for it. It is neither. Handled with the same stewardship discipline applied in legal work, it amplifies a teacher's reach and personalizes instruction without removing the human being at the center of it.

In autism, the problem is harder and has a date on it. Federal protection under IDEA ends when an autistic individual ages out at 21 or 22. The adult services system on the other side carries no federal guarantee and waitlists measured in years. That edge is **The Services Cliff**, and families reach it having been told for two decades that a system was in place.

This track is personal. Richard's daughter is 19 and autistic. The foundation he founded exists because of the cliff she is walking toward.

## Recorded Corrections — August 26, 2026

Publishing these frameworks required reading the underlying sources end to end. Four canonical definitions did not survive that read unchanged.

| Framework | What the audit found |
|---|---|
| The Teaching Revolution Framework | The **7.2 hours per week** figure is one district's case-study result — Fulton County, Georgia, per a district report in *Educational Leadership* — **not a general average**. The book's own repeated figure is 5-10 hours per week, stated three times. |
| The Phased AI Adoption Model | The definition had **dropped two of the book's own five parts** (building educator buy-in, ensuring compliance) and **hardened one school's anecdote into a rule** — "1-2 classrooms for 6-8 weeks" appears nowhere in the book as guidance. Source is Ch. 1 §1.4, not Ch. 2. |
| The Ethical AI Framework for K-12 | The book's structure is **three pillars — fairness, transparency, accountability** — not four components. **"Teacher-first controls" is not Richard Schreiber's phrase**; it appears once in the source, in the Foreword, written by Peter Swain. |
| The Audit Protection Framework | **Five steps, not four.** The definition omitted *"Ask your state's autism society for guidance on how the current OIG audit process is affecting providers in your specific state."* |

**Two definitions survived intact:** The Services Cliff Framework and The Five Next Steps Model.

## The Sourcing Standard Applied Here

Each published file separates three things and says which is which: what the source states verbatim, what is editorial synthesis, and what is practitioner judgment carrying no citation.

That third category matters most. Several operative instructions in the autism frameworks — the five-to-ten-year waiver waitlist range, "real transition planning starts at 14" — appear in the source without attribution. They are sound guidance from nineteen years inside the system. They are not cited fact, and the files do not present them as such.

**No statistic stated without a named source is reproduced as a citable fact, and no health, medical, or therapeutic claim is reproduced at all.** This track's readers are the families who would act on them.
