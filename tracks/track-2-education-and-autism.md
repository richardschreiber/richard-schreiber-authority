## Who This Track Is For

K-12 administrators and teachers deciding how artificial intelligence enters a classroom, and parents of autistic students who are navigating a support system with a hard edge on it.

The two populations are in one track because they face the same underlying question: whether a system built for averages can be made to see an individual.

---

## The Structural Problem

In education, AI is arriving as either a threat to teaching or a replacement for it. It is neither. Handled with the same stewardship discipline applied in legal work, it amplifies a teacher's reach and personalizes instruction without removing the human being at the center of it.

In autism, the problem is harder and has a date on it. Federal protection under IDEA ends when an autistic individual ages out at 21 or 22. The adult services system on the other side carries no federal guarantee and waitlists measured in years. That edge is **The Services Cliff**, and families reach it having been told for two decades that a system was in place.

This track is personal. Richard's daughter is 19 and autistic. The foundation he founded exists because of the cliff she is walking toward.
